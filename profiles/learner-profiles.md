## Priya PostDoc

Priya is a postdoctoral researcher in a wet-lab group that recently started
generating its own sequencing data. She is comfortable interpreting summary
statistics and plots from a bioinformatics core, but has never looked at raw
aligned reads or variant calls herself. A recent RNA-seq experiment produced
a result she doesn't fully trust, and she wants to be able to check it
herself rather than only relying on a collaborator's pipeline output.

This course will teach Priya how to load her own BAM and VCF files into IGV
and interpret what she sees well enough to sanity-check a pipeline result.
She is most interested in the alignment/SNP and RNA-seq episodes. After the
workshop, Priya will be able to load her lab's sequencing data into IGV,
navigate to a specific gene or variant of interest, and judge for herself
whether a candidate variant or splicing event looks convincing or looks like
an artifact.

## Sam Sysadmin-Turned-Analyst

Sam has a background in IT and recently moved into a bioinformatics support
role. He is very comfortable with computers and file formats in the
abstract, but has limited biology background and has never used a genome
browser. He needs to be able to help researchers troubleshoot "why doesn't my
file load in IGV" and similar questions.

This course will give Sam a working mental model of how IGV organizes data
(genomes, tracks, file formats) so that he can reason about and debug loading
problems. He is particularly interested in the file-format overview and the
"load your own genome" episode. After the workshop, Sam will be able to
identify which IGV menu to use for a given file type, explain why a genome
loaded from a bare FASTA file has no gene names, and guide a researcher
through loading their own data.

## Alex Analyst

Alex is a master's student doing a genomics-focused research project. They
have taken an introductory bioinformatics course and are comfortable with the
command line, but their course covered alignment and variant calling only as
command-line tools, without much emphasis on visual inspection. Their
supervisor has asked them to visually confirm a handful of candidate variants
before including them in a manuscript.

This course will give Alex the specific IGV skills needed to inspect
candidate SNPs and structural signals by eye. They are most interested in the
alignment/SNP episode and the wrap-up's pointer to structural variants. After
the workshop, Alex will be able to load a BAM file alongside a list of
candidate variants, use sorting and coloring options to judge whether each
variant looks genuine, and communicate that judgment (with a screenshot) to
their supervisor.
